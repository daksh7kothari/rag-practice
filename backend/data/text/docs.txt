# SRM Team Robocon Overview

SRM Team Robocon is the official robotics team of SRM Institute of Science and Technology. The team participates in national and international robotics competitions and develops robots to solve competition-specific challenges.

The team consists of software, electronics, mechanical, and management domains that collaborate throughout the robot development lifecycle.

The software domain focuses on programming, algorithms, computer vision, autonomous navigation, ROS, path planning, embedded programming, machine learning, sensor fusion, localization, and control systems.

The electronics domain focuses on PCB design, microcontrollers, motor drivers, power electronics, battery management, communication protocols, sensor integration, debugging, and electrical system reliability.

The mechanical domain focuses on CAD design, mechanism development, structural design, manufacturing, assembly, transmission systems, pneumatics, material selection, and robot optimization.

The management domain focuses on sponsorship, social media, content creation, documentation, logistics, finance, outreach, and event coordination.

The team regularly works with technologies such as Python, C++, Arduino, STM32, ESP32, ROS, OpenCV, YOLO, Fusion 360, SolidWorks, Git, Linux, and various robotics simulation tools.

Robocon robots often use sensors such as IMUs, encoders, cameras, ultrasonic sensors, LiDAR sensors, limit switches, and proximity sensors.

Common actuators used in robotics include DC motors, servo motors, stepper motors, pneumatic cylinders, and linear actuators.

Autonomous robots rely on perception, decision making, localization, path planning, and control algorithms.

Computer vision is used for object detection, target tracking, marker detection, localization, obstacle detection, and autonomous navigation.

Embedded systems are responsible for sensor reading, motor control, communication, and real-time decision making.

Communication protocols commonly used in robotics include UART, I2C, SPI, CAN, Ethernet, and wireless communication methods.

Control systems commonly used in robotics include PID control, feedback loops, motion profiling, trajectory following, and state machines.

Mechanical design often involves linkages, gear trains, belt drives, chain drives, lead screws, rack and pinion systems, and custom fabricated mechanisms.

Robot development involves ideation, design, prototyping, manufacturing, testing, debugging, iteration, and final validation.

Members are expected to work collaboratively across domains rather than operating in isolation.

Problem solving, teamwork, ownership, discipline, consistency, and willingness to learn are highly valued within the team.

New members are typically trained through workshops, projects, mentoring sessions, and hands-on robot development activities.

The recruitment process may include aptitude tests, technical assessments, interviews, group discussions, practical tasks, or domain-specific evaluations.

Candidates are not always expected to possess prior robotics experience. Strong fundamentals and learning attitude are often considered more important.

Software candidates may be asked questions related to programming, data structures, algorithms, computer vision, Linux, Git, Python, C++, or robotics fundamentals.

Electronics candidates may be asked questions related to circuits, sensors, microcontrollers, communication protocols, and troubleshooting.

Mechanical candidates may be asked questions related to CAD, manufacturing processes, machine elements, mechanisms, and engineering fundamentals.

Management candidates may be asked questions related to communication, organization, sponsorship, content creation, event planning, and leadership.

Robocon competitions emphasize innovation, engineering excellence, strategy, teamwork, reliability, and efficient problem solving.

Team members often participate in brainstorming sessions, design reviews, technical discussions, testing sessions, and competition preparation activities.

A successful robot must balance speed, accuracy, robustness, maintainability, safety, and compliance with competition rules.

Documentation is an important part of engineering and includes design reports, technical specifications, manufacturing drawings, testing records, and competition submissions.

Safety practices are critical during manufacturing, electrical integration, testing, and competition operation.

Version control systems such as Git are commonly used to manage software development and collaboration.

Simulation tools may be used to validate robot designs before physical manufacturing.

Competition preparation often includes repeated testing, subsystem validation, autonomous tuning, driver practice, and failure analysis.

Members gain practical experience in engineering design, project management, communication, teamwork, and real-world problem solving through participation in the team.

The primary goal of the team is to build competitive robots while helping students develop technical skills, leadership qualities, and engineering experience.
